I didn't notice it then—the way my cousin's shadow seemed to pool unnaturally against the rusted earth, darker than the smog-choked sky above. A subtle, rotting chill clung to the periphery of the Reality Marble, a whisper of Hassan-i-Sabbah waiting patiently for the King's distraction.

## Hour 63: The Evolved Shadow

Across the wasteland, my cousin was panicking. The fifty-million-dollar god he had purchased was being matched by a man in a red coat. The cousin's arrogant facade crumbled into pure, unadulterated terror.

<SystemWindow title="COUSIN">"Kill him! Just use the sword that destroys worlds! I command you! Use the Seals!"</SystemWindow>

He raised his right hand, the three crimson Command Seals glowing with a toxic light. Gilgamesh turned his head, his crimson eyes narrowing with absolute, murderous disgust. He would never allow a rat to force him to draw *Ea*.

But the cousin never finished the incantation.

                Assassin — 2nd Evolutionary Form
                Hassan-i-Sabbah

The unnaturally dark shadow cast by his own boots suddenly twisted, surging upward and elongating across the dirt. The ambient temperature plummeted. A foul, suffocating miasma of death poured from the darkness.

Rising from the shadow was not a normal Servant. It was an anomaly—an Assassin that had achieved its 2nd-stage evolutionary form. It was a towering, wraith-like monstrosity draped in tattered bandages and bone-white armor, radiating a curse so dense it made the very ground rot beneath it. The corrupted Hassan-i-Sabbah had been waiting for the exact moment the master's panic left him vulnerable.

Before anyone could react, the Assassin's elongated, clawed hand swept upward.

The cousin's head detached from his shoulders. Blood sprayed across the rusted swords as his body collapsed, the smartphone he had been clutching tumbling into the dirt.

<SystemWindow title="ASSASSIN">"...The false master is severed... the shadow claims the toll..."</SystemWindow>

The entity's voice was a chorus of a thousand dying echoes.

Gilgamesh leaped backward, narrowly avoiding a cursed strike from the wraith that instantly decayed a dozen swords in its path.

The sky of gears shattered like a mirror struck by a hammer.

The wasteland dissolved, and the suffocating atmosphere of the high-tech Himalayan bunker rushed back into our lungs.

The smartphone. It was still recording.

He had been live-streaming it to the cloud.

The raw footage had already been ripped, clipped, and mirrored across ten thousand servers.

I pulled out my own phone, which I had powered off in the SUV. I turned it on. A flood of thousands of notifications instantly froze the device for a solid ten seconds. When it finally loaded, I opened a news app. The "Shoe Video" was gone.

And hidden behind the military's geopolitical panic, the Clock Tower's leyline monitors would be screaming. The masquerade of the Magi was breached.

We were no longer a meme. We were a planetary target.

## Hour 66: The Town of Traitors

We fled the bunker before the dawn broke. Archer needed medical-grade supplies and high-density mana gems to stabilize his core, forcing us to detour into a populated Himalayan border town nestled in the valleys below.

Saber and Archer dematerialized their armor, wearing civilian clothing we salvaged from the SUV. I pulled a hood low over my face. We needed to be ghosts. We walked into the crowded marketplace, the morning fog thick and biting.

But the digital age leaves no shadows to hide in.

I was standing near a pharmacy stall when a young man holding a phone pointed at me. His eyes went wide. Then, he didn't run. He yelled.

<SystemWindow title="CIVILIAN">"It's him! The terrorist from the video! The freak with the monsters!"</SystemWindow>

The marketplace went dead silent. Then, the greed and the fear took over. The news had placed a massive, unprecedented bounty on my head. These were the "innocents" Saber had sworn to protect. They grabbed iron pipes, butcher knives, and heavy stones. A mob of fifty people surrounded us, their faces twisted with hatred and opportunism.

A stone was thrown. It struck my shoulder, hard enough to bruise the bone.

<SystemWindow title="SABER">"Stop this! We do not wish to harm you!"</SystemWindow>

Saber stepped in front of me, refusing to draw her sword. She raised her arms, taking the brunt of the blows. A man swung a rusted pipe at her head; she caught it bare-handed, pushing him away gently. She was bleeding from a cut on her cheek, taking the abuse of the mortals she revered.

Then, the chopping sound of helicopter blades drowned out the mob.

## Hour 67: The Slaughter of the Innocents

Three heavy military gunships crested the mountain ridge, hovering directly over the marketplace. Laser sights painted the crowd. The civilians stopped, cheering, thinking their protectors had arrived to arrest the anomalies.

A megaphone blared from the lead gunship.

<SystemWindow title="COMMANDER">"Targets acquired. The anomalies have engaged the civilian populace. Weapons free! Eradicate the crowd, but secure the anomalies alive. R&D wants the dimensional technology intact. Fire!"</SystemWindow>

The civilians didn't even have time to scream.

The gunships opened fire with heavy autocannons. They didn't aim for Saber or me. They aimed at the crowd, using the sheer volume of explosive rounds to turn the marketplace into a meat grinder. The military didn't care about collateral damage; they cared about securing the narrative and stealing the magic. To them, these people were just acceptable losses to frame us.

Blood painted the snow. The people who had just been trying to kill me were now being ripped apart by their own government.

<SystemWindow title="SABER">"NO!"</SystemWindow>

The firing ceased. The marketplace was a graveyard of smoke, fire, and torn bodies.

Saber knelt in the bloody snow, her civilian clothes shredded, shielding the surviving boy beneath her arms. She looked down at him, her emerald eyes wide with desperate empathy, reaching out a trembling, bloodstained hand to comfort him.
