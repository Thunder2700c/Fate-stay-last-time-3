07 / Questions Project information

A few things worth knowing.

Why is the reading room empty?

The novel is being rewritten from the first page. The archive will open when the new chapters are ready; older drafts will not be republished as placeholders.

Are these pages three different websites?

They are three independent homepage documents with separate compositions for phone, tablet, and desktop. They share the same project identity and core content.

How is the layout selected?

The site selects a composition from the current viewport width. It does not claim to detect the physical device. Resizing across a breakpoint can load a different page.

Do the reader controls affect chapters?

Not yet. The typography lab is a working preview. Its preferences are stored locally and demonstrate the controls intended for the future chapter reader.

Does the site require animation?

No. Motion is an enhancement. The content and navigation remain available if GSAP does not load or reduced motion is enabled.

Where are my reader preferences saved?

The preview uses local browser storage on this device. Clearing site data or using another browser may reset those settings.

Can I read the old version here?

No. This edition is a clean restart. Previous drafts and their story material are intentionally not carried into the new archive.

When will the first chapter be published?

There is no confirmed publication date. The archive status will change only after the author has supplied and approved the rewritten material.

11 / Device questions 10 ANSWERS

Before you settle in.

Does the side rail replace the header?

No. It provides section shortcuts and supplements the main document navigation.

Are reader settings synced across browsers?

No. The settings preview stores preferences locally and does not provide account synchronization.

Can I print chapters?

Chapter print styling will be considered when the rewritten manuscript and real chapter pages exist.

Why is the archive empty?

The project is being rewritten from page one and no approved chapter content is available yet.

Does a larger monitor make the page faster?

Not automatically. Performance depends on browser, hardware, assets, and rendering workload.

What happens if JavaScript is blocked?

The static page remains available, while the field-guide filters and reader preview enhancements will not operate.

Is the manifest enough to install an app?

A manifest alone does not make this a complete offline PWA. No service worker or offline chapter cache is claimed.

Why is the sitemap not listing desktop and tablet?

Those are viewport-specific variants with the same homepage canonical URL, so the sitemap lists the canonical homepage.

Does the site track my reading?

No account or chapter history feature is implemented. Only local preview preferences may be stored.

Where can I report a broken link?

The contact page identifies the public project repository and explains the current contact limitation.

These scenarios turn the layout principles into concrete checks. They describe browser and accessibility situations that should be tested before the site is called production-ready.

A reader opens a deep link

A desktop visitor may arrive at a specific section from search or a shared URL.

The target section needs a descriptive heading and stable ID.

Validation step

Expected outcome
The visitor lands at the requested section and can identify its context.

READING SCENARIO 02

A reader uses multiple monitors

The browser may be moved between displays with different pixel densities.

The layout should rely on CSS viewport dimensions rather than assumptions about physical pixels.

Validation step
Move the window between displays and resize it around the documented breakpoints.
Expected outcome
The composition changes only when the viewport requires it.

READING SCENARIO 03

A reader uses a screen reader

A visual side rail does not automatically provide a meaningful navigation landmark.

Use semantic nav labels and a logical heading sequence.

Validation step
Inspect the accessibility tree and navigate by headings.
Expected outcome
The page outline remains useful without visual styling.

READING SCENARIO 04

A reader prints a project page

A printout should not include fixed controls or decorative progress elements.

Keep the document content in normal flow and avoid making text part of an image.

Validation step
Use print preview to inspect the page structure.
Expected outcome
The project information remains legible on paper.

READING SCENARIO 05

A reader opens a narrow desktop window

A desktop browser window may be resized below the width of a typical monitor.

The page must adapt instead of preserving a wide rail at all costs.

Validation step
Resize the window through the tablet breakpoint and inspect navigation.
Expected outcome
The side rail yields to the narrower composition cleanly.

A reader blocks third-party scripts

Privacy extensions and network filters may block GSAP or other external resources.

Core content must not depend on those scripts to become visible.

Validation step

The reader can still navigate and read all static project information.
