## Hour 25 to 28: Structural Grasp and the Digital Forge

We abandoned the basement and moved upstairs to my bedroom, the nerve center of my previous, quiet life. The sun had set over Ludhiana, plunging the city back into its smoggy, neon-lit night. My room, usually a sanctuary for web development and writing *The Gods Rank Awakening*, now served as the war room for the end of the world.

Archer looked around with a detached, cynical curiosity. He noted the Fate poster on the wall, the empty coffee mugs, and finally, my dual-monitor desktop setup humming in the corner.

<SystemWindow title="ARCHER">"A crude forge. But the underlying principle of a network is no different from the leylines of a territory. It is all just pathways of energy."</SystemWindow>

<SystemWindow title="GURTEJ">"Can you trace it?"</SystemWindow>

I sat in my chair and pulled up the IP logs I had managed to scrape before the syndication networks blocked my ping requests.

Archer didn't ask for the keyboard. He reached out his tanned, scarred hand and placed it flat against the side of the PC casing.

<Chant font="cinzel">"Trace On."</Chant>

Faint, glowing green circuits resembling microscopic circuitry crawled up his arm, reflecting in his steely eyes. Archer's Structural Grasp didn't just read the physical makeup of a sword—it comprehended the entire history, composition, and conceptual purpose of any object he touched. Right now, he was grasping the architecture of the modern internet.

For three hours, the room was dead silent except for the whirring of the CPU fan. Archer stood like a statue, his mind projecting across fiber-optic cables, traversing the globe at the speed of light. He bypassed firewalls not by guessing passwords, but by conceptually understanding the flaws in their coding.

At Hour 27, the numbers on my screen stopped moving.

The view count on the primary video, which had been spinning like a slot machine, froze.

1,104,560,000 views — FROZEN

<SystemWindow title="ARCHER">"I have located the primary server nodes hosting the visual data. And I have traced the financial conduits. The 'Mora' he has gathered... it flows through a dozen digital banks."</SystemWindow>

<SystemWindow title="GURTEJ">"Burn it."</SystemWindow>

My voice was completely devoid of its usual hesitation. The mediator was dead.

<SystemWindow title="ARCHER">"With pleasure."</SystemWindow>

He didn't inject prana directly into the software—raw magical energy and modern electronics despise each other. Doing so would have instantly fried my desktop into slag. Instead, he used Structural Grasp to comprehend the physical architecture of the global network, then manipulated the components at the molecular level.

In server farms located in California, Dublin, and Tokyo, he forced capacitors to overload. He commanded cooling fans to seize. He warped hard drive platters by microscopic margins. He wasn't hacking the code; he was assassinating the hardware.

At Hour 28, my cousin's bank accounts, crypto wallets, and ad-sense revenue streams were systematically drained to absolute zero. Archer didn't steal the money; he conceptually erased the ledgers.

Revenue: $140,000 → $0.00

Crypto Wallets: ERASED

Ad-Sense: TERMINATED

## Hour 29 to 32: The Panic and the Mercenaries

I opened my phone and went to my cousin's Instagram. He was live.

He was sitting in an upscale hotel room—he had already left town, likely to celebrate his sudden wealth. He was mid-sentence, laughing with his viewers, when his expression suddenly faltered. His eyes darted off-screen.

<SystemWindow title="COUSIN">"Wait, what?"</SystemWindow>

The live chat was flooding with confused messages.

Viewer: "Bro, the video is down."

Viewer: "Link is dead."

Viewer: "Did you delete it?"

My cousin picked up a secondary phone. His face drained of color. He tapped furiously, his breathing picking up.

<SystemWindow title="COUSIN">"No, no, no... the bank app is down. Everything is... it says zero."</SystemWindow>

I watched the sheer, unadulterated terror wash over the man who had ruined my life. It was intoxicating. But a rat backed into a corner will always bite.

He ended the stream abruptly. What he did next, I pieced together from the network traffic Archer was monitoring. My cousin had connections—shady ones he had bragged about at family gatherings. Using a hidden, offline cache of funds he hadn't digitized yet, he made a frantic call to the dark web. He believed he was under a massive, coordinated cyber-attack by a rival syndicate or a disgruntled hacker group.

He hired a PMC—a Private Military Company that operated off the books.

But there was a complication he didn't foresee. The Clock Tower had orbital satellites and leyline monitors. They had detected the massive surge of True Ether from my basement when Saber and Archer were summoned. They knew a Holy Grail-class event had occurred in Ludhiana.

The Clock Tower Enforcers intercepted my cousin's PMC contract. They took over the operation. They weren't coming for my cousin's money; they were coming to secure the Master who had illegally summoned Heroic Spirits.

<SystemWindow title="SABER">"Master. We are no longer alone."</SystemWindow>

## Hour 33 to 36: The Siege of Ludhiana

It was 2:00 AM. The heavy, oppressive heat of the Punjab night hung over my street.

I looked out the window. The neighborhood was dead quiet. The stray dogs that usually roamed the alleys were gone, their instincts warning them of the unnatural presence approaching.

Four matte-black SUVs rolled down my narrow street, their headlights extinguished. They moved with absolute, terrifying precision. They parked at the four corners of my property, boxing my house in.

<SystemWindow title="ARCHER">"Tactical operatives. Armed with modern kinetic weapons. But there are others. Three distinct prana signatures. Magi. Clock Tower hounds."</SystemWindow>

<SystemWindow title="GURTEJ">"Saber."</SystemWindow>

My heart was hammering against my ribs, the reality of physical violence finally crashing into my digital warfare.

<SystemWindow title="SABER">"Do not fear, Master. They are merely men. I am a Knight."</SystemWindow>

She didn't draw her sword, but the air around her swirled, a miniature cyclone forming in the cramped bedroom. Her blue and silver armor materialized over her casual clothing with a flash of light.

At Hour 34, they breached.

A bounded field was thrown over my house, cutting off all sound from the outside world. To our neighbors, my house would appear completely silent, even as all hell broke loose.

<Shake><Scream>CRASH.</Scream></Shake>

The front door was blown off its hinges by a shaped charge. Flashbangs were tossed into the living room, filling the ground floor with blinding light and deafening noise. Six PMC operatives clad in heavy tactical gear stormed in, assault rifles raised, sweeping the rooms with laser sights.

Saber didn't use the stairs. She stepped to the floorboards of my bedroom, gathered prana into her armored boots, and plummeted straight through the floor, shattering the wood and plaster to land directly in the center of the living room.

The operatives opened fire. The deafening roar of automatic gunfire filled the house.

<Shake><Scream>BRRRRRRT!</Scream></Shake>

But the bullets never touched her.

Invisible Air acted as a flawless, impenetrable shield. The high-caliber rounds struck the compressed wall of cyclone-force wind surrounding her and were deflected, ricocheting harmlessly into the walls and ceiling.

The operatives froze, their tactical training completely useless against a phenomenon that defied physics.

Saber moved. To the human eye, it was just a blur of silver. She didn't use the blade; it would be a dishonor to cut down mere mortals with a holy sword. She used the flat of the invisible blade and her gauntleted fists.

In less than three seconds, all six men were unconscious on the floor, their armor dented by blows that carried the weight of a freight train.

<SystemWindow title="SABER">"The vanguard is subdued."</SystemWindow>

But then, the front wall of my living room simply... dissolved.

The three Clock Tower Enforcers stepped through the rubble. They wore long, dark coats, holding mystic codes—staves and jeweled daggers that hummed with stored magical energy.

<SystemWindow title="ENFORCER">"Surrender the Servants, boy. You are an unregistered anomaly. By order of the Mage's Association, this territory is forfeit."</SystemWindow>

I walked to the edge of the shattered floorhole, looking down at the Enforcers. Archer stood behind me, manifesting his black bow.

<SystemWindow title="GURTEJ">"They don't understand, do they?"</SystemWindow>

<SystemWindow title="ARCHER">"Magi rarely do. They think they are dealing with a rogue spellcaster. They don't realize they are standing on ground zero of a timeline erasure."</SystemWindow>
