07 / Questions Project information

A few things worth knowing.

Why is the reading room empty?

The novel is being rewritten from the first page. The archive will open when the new chapters are ready; older drafts will not be republished as placeholders.

Are these pages three different websites?

They are three independent homepage documents with separate compositions for phone, tablet, and desktop. They share the same project identity and core content.

How is the layout selected?

The site selects a composition from the current viewport width. It does not claim to detect the physical device. Resizing across a breakpoint can load a different page.

Do the reader controls affect chapters?

Not yet. The typography lab is a working preview. Its preferences are stored locally and demonstrate the controls intended for the future chapter reader.

Does the site require animation?

No. Motion is an enhancement. The content and navigation remain available if GSAP does not load or reduced motion is enabled.

Where are my reader preferences saved?

The preview uses local browser storage on this device. Clearing site data or using another browser may reset those settings.

Can I read the old version here?

No. This edition is a clean restart. Previous drafts and their story material are intentionally not carried into the new archive.

When will the first chapter be published?

There is no confirmed publication date. The archive status will change only after the author has supplied and approved the rewritten material.

11 / Device questions 10 ANSWERS

Before you settle in.

Can I read chapters offline?

Offline chapter access is not implemented. The current site is static and network-served.

Does the site remember my reading position?

No chapter history is stored. Only reader-preview display preferences can be saved locally.

Why is the chapter list empty?

The manuscript is being rewritten. No chapter titles or excerpts are invented to fill the archive.

Will the page work without GSAP?

Yes. GSAP adds optional motion; the document content and normal navigation remain in the HTML.

What is saved in my browser?

The reader preview may save tone, text size, line spacing, reading width, and font choice in localStorage.

Are preferences shared with other devices?

No. Browser storage is local to the current browser profile and does not synchronize.

Can I use the site with a keyboard?

Native links, buttons, inputs, and disclosure elements support keyboard interaction. Full assistive-technology QA remains pending.

Why does the page change after rotating?

The router chooses a document from viewport width and preserves the current section hash.

Does the site use analytics?

The inspected project code does not intentionally add an analytics platform or advertising pixel.

Can I send a private message here?

No private contact inbox or form endpoint has been configured.

13 / Reading scenarios MOBILE / REAL-WORLD USE

The page meets real readers.

These scenarios turn the layout principles into concrete checks. They describe browser and accessibility situations that should be tested before the site is called production-ready.

READING SCENARIO 01

A visitor arrives from a shared link

A phone visitor may land halfway down the page from a message or bookmark.

Keep section headings descriptive and make the skip link available at the start of the document.

Validation step
Open a direct hash URL on a narrow phone and verify that the target is not hidden by the header.
Expected outcome
The visitor can identify the section and continue without restarting the page.

READING SCENARIO 02

A visitor reads in bright daylight

Outdoor glare changes the perceived contrast of fine text and thin borders.

Do not rely on faint metadata to communicate important status.

Validation step
Review the main text and controls at high screen brightness and default zoom.
Expected outcome
The archive status and navigation remain readable.
MOBILE / EDITION 03 SCENARIO 2 OF 6

READING SCENARIO 03

A visitor uses a screen magnifier

Magnification reduces the amount of visible content at once.

Avoid fixed overlays that cover the current reading position.

Validation step
Test with browser zoom and a screen magnifier where available.
Expected outcome
The visitor can follow the document without horizontal panning.
MOBILE / EDITION 03 SCENARIO 3 OF 6

READING SCENARIO 04

A visitor has limited data

Remote fonts and decorative resources can add network requests.

The page should communicate its project status before every optional asset loads.

Validation step
Block external resources and confirm that core text remains visible.
Expected outcome
The first meaningful content is available without waiting for animation.
MOBILE / EDITION 03 SCENARIO 4 OF 6

READING SCENARIO 05

A visitor uses text-to-speech

Semantic structure helps speech tools move through a long document.

Visual labels should correspond to meaningful headings and landmarks.

Validation step
Inspect heading order and landmark names with an accessibility tree.
Expected outcome
The document has a coherent spoken outline.

READING SCENARIO 06

A visitor returns after a pause

A reader may switch apps and return to the same page later.

Avoid timers and automatic route changes that interrupt a reading session.

Validation step
Leave the page in the background, return, and inspect the current position.
Expected outcome
The page does not unexpectedly reset or move the visitor.
