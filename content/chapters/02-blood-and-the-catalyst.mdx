## Hour 13 to 14: The Architecture of Vengeance

The basement beneath my house was a relic of unfinished construction, a cavern of exposed brick, dusty concrete, and the lingering smell of damp earth. It was completely isolated from the suffocating heat of the Ludhiana afternoon above. Down here, the world felt distant. The incessant buzzing of my phone, the notifications, the billion pairs of eyes—none of it could penetrate the concrete.

I knelt in the center of the floor. My hands were shaking, not from fear, but from a profound, chilling exhaustion.

The catalyst lay before me: the ruined golden jutti. It was no longer just a piece of torn leather; it was an artifact imbued with the conceptual weight of absolute humiliation. In the rules of magecraft, an object gathers power based on the human emotions tied to it. This shoe was currently the focal point of a billion laughing minds. It was heavy with a dark, toxic gravity.

I didn't have liquid mercury or crushed gems. I had myself. I took the pocketknife and pressed it against my palm. The sting was sharp, grounding me. I let the blood drip onto the dusty concrete, dragging my finger to draw the summoning circle. I traced the intricate geometric patterns I had memorized from my obsessions, marrying the logic of web architecture with the chaotic lines of an arcane array.

My blood mixed with the dust. It wasn't perfect, but it was fueled by a desperation that defied logic. I stepped back, my chest heaving. The air in the basement was already growing heavy, the atmospheric pressure dropping as if a storm were brewing directly overhead.

I closed my eyes. I didn't reach out to the Root or the traditional magic circuits—I didn't have them. Instead, I reached inward, into the core of my own shattered ideals, into the absolute rejection of the reality above me.

## Hour 15: The Manifestation of Myth

The explosion of mana didn't just rattle the basement; it shook the foundation of the house. A blinding pillar of azure and crimson light erupted from the circle, forcing me to shield my eyes. The sheer force of it blew the dust off the concrete, leaving the air tasting of ozone and ancient iron.

As the light faded, the heavy, suffocating silence returned, but it was different now. It was the silence of a predator entering a room.

The smoke cleared. Two figures stood within the boundaries of the blood-drawn circle.

The first was a woman. She was clad in silver armor that gleamed with an ethereal, unblemished light, over a dress of deep, royal blue. Her golden hair was tied back, and her emerald eyes locked onto mine with a piercing, sovereign intensity. She held her hands as if resting them on the pommel of a sword, but the blade itself was invisible, masked by swirling currents of wind. Artoria Pendragon. Saber. The King of Knights.

Beside her stood a man who looked like he had been forged in a dying star. He wore a dark, form-fitting breastplate and a vibrant, crimson shroud that seemed to absorb the dim light. His skin was tanned, his hair shock-white, and his steel-gray eyes scanned the basement with the cold, cynical calculation of a veteran assassin. EMIYA. Archer. The Wrought Iron Hero.

Saber stepped forward, the steel of her boots clinking against the concrete.

<SystemWindow title="GURTEJ">"I am."</SystemWindow>

## Hour 16 to 18: The Briefing of the Viral War

I didn't try to stand. I sat on a wooden crate, the adrenaline crash leaving me dizzy.

<SystemWindow title="GURTEJ">"There is no Holy Grail. Not here. There are no other Masters. There is no Clock Tower oversight."</SystemWindow>

Saber frowned, her invisible blade shifting slightly.

<SystemWindow title="SABER">"Then why have we been called from the Throne of Heroes? A Heroic Spirit is a weapon of humanity's defense. If there is no war, if there is no threat to the Human Order..."</SystemWindow>

<SystemWindow title="GURTEJ">"The Human Order *is* the threat."</SystemWindow>

I pulled out my smartphone—the device I had dreaded looking at for the past eighteen hours. I unlocked it and placed it on the crate between us. I brought up the video. The analytics. The comments.

<SystemWindow title="GURTEJ">"Thirteen hours ago, my shoe broke at a wedding. My cousin recorded it. He uploaded it to a global digital network. Right now, over one billion people are watching it. They are laughing. He is making a fortune off of my absolute humiliation. My life, my future, my dignity—it was all erased for a joke."</SystemWindow>

Saber stared at the small screen. Her brow furrowed in deep confusion.

<SystemWindow title="SABER">"A... moving picture? Sent across the world? And this... 'billion' people... they mock you for a failure of equipment? Where is the honor in this? Where is the challenge?"</SystemWindow>

<SystemWindow title="ARCHER">"There is none."</SystemWindow>

He had stepped closer, his gray eyes reading the screen, analyzing the engagement metrics and the syndication networks. As a Counter Guardian from the modern era, he understood exactly what he was looking at.

<SystemWindow title="ARCHER">"It's an algorithmic slaughter. A digital crucifixion."</SystemWindow>

He looked at me, his gaze piercing through my skull.

<SystemWindow title="ARCHER">"You didn't summon us to fight a Magus. You summoned us to fight a server farm. You want us to kill a meme."</SystemWindow>

<SystemWindow title="GURTEJ">"No. I want you to kill the world that made it."</SystemWindow>

## Hour 19 to 24: The Contract of Erasure

Silence stretched across the basement.

<SystemWindow title="SABER">"You ask me to destroy the innocent because the guilty walk among them? That is not justice, Master. That is a massacre. I am a Knight. I will not turn my blade against humanity over a wounded pride."</SystemWindow>

<SystemWindow title="GURTEJ">"Are they innocent? A billion people watched a man's dignity burn and turned it into entertainment. Not one person in a billion said 'stop.' Silence in the face of cruelty is complicity. You know this, King of Knights. Camelot didn't fall because of one traitor. It fell because the rest of the kingdom watched it happen."</SystemWindow>

Saber flinched. It was barely perceptible—a tightening of the jaw, a fractional shift in her grip on the invisible blade. But I saw it. I had found the crack in the armor of the King.

<SystemWindow title="ARCHER">"A compelling argument, Master. But there is a mechanical flaw in your grand execution. Look at your hand. You have no Command Seals. No enforcement mechanism. If Saber or I decide to walk away right now, there is absolutely nothing binding us to you."</SystemWindow>

<SystemWindow title="GURTEJ">"Then walk."</SystemWindow>

Archer raised an eyebrow, genuinely caught off guard.

<SystemWindow title="GURTEJ">"I'm serious. I won't force you. I won't trick you. If you stay, it's because you agree that a world built on this kind of cruelty deserves what's coming. That's the only contract I'm offering."</SystemWindow>

Saber was silent for a long time. When she spoke again, her voice was quieter. Not defeated, but resolved.

<SystemWindow title="SABER">"If this world has truly abandoned its honor... I will be your sword, Gurtej. But know this—if I find even one soul worth saving before the end, I will stay my blade."</SystemWindow>

<SystemWindow title="ARCHER">"...A contract of conviction rather than compulsion. How absurdly idealistic. Fine. I've followed worse reasons to the end of the world."</SystemWindow>

I looked at the terminal on my laptop upstairs in my mind's eye.

<SystemWindow title="GURTEJ">"First, we blind them. Archer, you are going to rip the cousin's digital empire to shreds. You are going to burn his servers to the ground."</SystemWindow>
