## Hour 49: The Meteor of Avalon

To witness a Heroic Spirit in true combat is to understand the terrifying insignificance of modern man.

I watched from the freezing ridge as Saber descended. She did not fall; she struck. The automated turrets swiveled their heavy barrels toward her, vomiting a continuous, deafening stream of high-caliber lead. The tracers lit up the Himalayan night like a swarm of angry, mechanical fireflies.

It meant nothing.

Saber's Prana Burst hit. Azure mana cycloned around her in a violent sheath, and the bullets simply died—caught in the conceptual pressure of her aura, their kinetic energy robbed, falling to the snow like dead insects. She struck the ground with the force of a localized earthquake. The snowpack exploded outward in a fifty-foot radius, revealing the bare, frozen rock beneath.

She stood before the massive, steel blast doors of my cousin's bunker. They were a meter thick, designed to withstand a direct tactical nuclear strike.

Saber didn't even drop into a combat stance. She raised both gauntleted hands and placed them flat against the freezing steel.

<SystemWindow title="SABER">"Begone."</SystemWindow>

She didn't swing a sword. She released the compressed prana within her core directly into the molecular structure of the doors. The steel groaned—a horrific, shrieking sound of metal being forced beyond its breaking point—and then blew inward. The doors crumpled like tin foil, tearing off their massive hydraulic hinges and crashing into the dark tunnel beyond, taking the inner sentry guns with them.

Silence descended over the valley, broken only by the howling of the mountain wind.

<SystemWindow title="ARCHER">"The path is clear, Master. But the atmosphere... it has changed."</SystemWindow>

He spoke softly from beside me, having dispersed his bow, his eyes fixed on the gaping maw of the fortress. I felt it too. I didn't have magic circuits, but the sheer density of the air making my teeth ache wasn't just the altitude. It felt like standing next to an open furnace. The ley lines of the mountain were screaming.

## Hour 50 to 52: The Descent into the Anomaly

We walked down the ridge and entered the bunker. The interior was a stark contrast to the brutalist exterior—a high-tech marvel of glass, LED lighting, and polished concrete. But the lights were flickering. The emergency alarms blared a dull, rhythmic red.

As we pushed deeper, stepping over the ruined husks of automated defense drones that Saber had casually dismantled, the feeling of wrongness intensified.

<SystemWindow title="SABER">"This is not just a bunker. The ambient mana is too thick. It is pooling here. Consolidating."</SystemWindow>

Her emerald eyes narrowed as she scanned the long corridors.

<SystemWindow title="ARCHER">"The Lesser Grail. Master, your summoning in Ludhiana was a massive, unregistered spike of True Ether. You didn't just summon two Servants; you acted as a conceptual flare. You woke up the dormant ley lines of the Himalayas. The World is trying to correct the anomaly by initiating a localized Holy Grail War."</SystemWindow>

A grim realization dawned on his face as he looked back at me.

<SystemWindow title="GURTEJ">"Meaning what?"</SystemWindow>

<SystemWindow title="ARCHER">"Meaning the rules of engagement have changed. And a Grail requires multiple Masters."</SystemWindow>

We reached the end of the corridor. A set of opulent, reinforced mahogany double doors stood incongruously in the center of the concrete. This was the inner sanctum.

I pushed them open.

## Hour 53: The Sanctum of Vanity

The room was vast, lined with banks of supercomputers and massive monitors. But the monitors were all dead, fried by Archer's earlier assault on the hardware. At the far end of the room, sitting in a plush leather executive chair, was my cousin.

He looked terrible. His designer suit was wrinkled, his hair was a mess, and his face was pale with a mixture of terror and sleep deprivation. But as we stepped into the room, a twisted, arrogant smirk slowly crawled across his face.

<SystemWindow title="COUSIN">"Gurtej. You actually came. You dragged a couple of cosplayers all the way to the top of the world because you couldn't handle a little joke."</SystemWindow>

His chuckle was dry and hysterical.

<SystemWindow title="GURTEJ">"The joke is over. Your servers are ashes. Your money is gone. The world is going to forget you ever existed. It's time to pay the toll."</SystemWindow>

I spoke coldly, walking toward him. My cousin laughed. He stood up, leaning against his desk. He raised his right hand.

Glowing on the back of his hand, burning with a toxic, crimson light, were three Command Seals shaped like a golden chalice.

My breath hitched.

<SystemWindow title="COUSIN">"You think you're the only one who can play this game? When my money started vanishing, I didn't just hire guns, Gurtej. I made a call to the dark web. A Magus in London was selling a relic—a piece of a fossilized snake skin. Cost me fifty million offline dollars. He said it was a 'catalyst' for a god. When the mountain started glowing an hour ago... I figured out what he meant."</SystemWindow>

The air in the room didn't just grow heavy; it completely vanished. The sheer pressure of the Mystery that filled the space forced me to my knees. Saber immediately stepped in front of me, manifesting her invisible blade, her posture snapping into a hyper-defensive stance.

<SystemWindow title="SABER">"Master, get back!"</SystemWindow>

Her voice was laced with a genuine, raw alarm I had never heard from her before.

Golden dust materialized in the air behind my cousin. It gathered, condensing into a swirling vortex of pure, arrogant light. The temperature spiked. The concrete floor cracked under the weight of the entity manifesting.

From the golden light stepped a man.

He possessed a beauty that was entirely inhuman, with crimson eyes that looked upon the world not as a home, but as a garden he owned. He wore gleaming, golden armor that radiated an ancient, oppressive majesty.

Gilgamesh. The King of Heroes.

## Hour 60: The Counter Guardian's Answer

Archer walked past me. He didn't have his bow. He didn't have his twin blades. He walked with a slow, deliberate cadence, straight into the lethal zone of the Gate of Babylon.

<SystemWindow title="SABER">"Archer, what are you doing?!"</SystemWindow>

<SystemWindow title="ARCHER">"I'm taking out the trash."</SystemWindow>

His voice was eerily calm.

Gilgamesh narrowed his crimson eyes, finally acknowledging the man in the red shroud.

<SystemWindow title="GILGAMESH">"You step forward to die, Faker? A nameless dog trying to bite a god?"</SystemWindow>

<SystemWindow title="ARCHER">"You talk too much, your Majesty. You rely on the weight of others' legends because you have none of your own to wield. You aren't a warrior. You're just a very wealthy thief."</SystemWindow>

<SystemWindow title="GILGAMESH">"I will turn you to ash, mongrel!"</SystemWindow>

It was a terrifying, sorrowful energy—the crystallized manifestation of a man who had been betrayed by his own ideals, who had slaughtered millions for the sake of a peace that never came.

He opened his eyes. They were not gray anymore; they burned with the light of a dying star.
